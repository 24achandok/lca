Objective: Evaluate the environmental impact of the portable cable machine across its full life cycle — from raw material extraction to end-of-life disposal. This assessment considers material choices, manufacturing processes, energy use, and recyclability to identify opportunities for sustainable design.

📦 Materials Used

  • 3D-printed PLA/PETG (main body)
  • Brass rod (guide shaft)
  • Aluminium bracket
  • Ash handle · Mahogany top
  • LCD 1602 · ESP32 · Servo · AA batteries

⚙️ Product Function

  • Portable resistance training
  • Adjustable cable tension via servo
  • LCD displays reps & resistance level
  • ESP32 enables Bluetooth / app control
⛏️

Raw Materials 1

Extraction & sourcing:

  • PLA – derived from corn starch (renewable, but land-use & fertiliser inputs)
  • PETG – petroleum-based, higher carbon footprint
  • Brass – copper & zinc mining, energy-intensive
  • Aluminium – bauxite mining, high embodied energy
  • Ash & Mahogany – sustainably sourced timber (FSC preferred)
  • Electronics – rare earth metals, silicon, lithium (batteries)
🌾 PLA🛢️ PETG🔩 Brass 🪵 Ash🌳 Mahogany🔋 Electronics
High impact: brass & electronics
🏭

Manufacturing 2

  • 3D printing – FDM process; electricity consumption (~0.5–1 kWh per part)
  • Brass rod – extruded and cut; low waste if accurately sized
  • Aluminium – CNC machined or laser-cut; coolant & energy use
  • Wood components – routed, sanded, finished with natural oil
  • PCB assembly – soldering, surface-mount components; flux & cleaning
  • Battery – AA batteries (alkaline) produced with high energy input
Medium impact: electricity & machining
🚚

Distribution & Use 3

  • Packaging – cardboard & recycled paper, minimal plastic
  • Transport – lightweight design reduces shipping emissions
  • Use phase – battery-powered (AA); servo & LCD draw ~200–300 mA
  • Durability – 3D-printed parts are repairable; brass & aluminium long-lasting
  • User maintenance – replace batteries; clean rod & bearings
  • Energy – no mains power required; low operational carbon
Low impact during use (battery only)
♻️

End of Life 4

  • PLA / PETG – recyclable (PLA industrial compostable, PETG recyclable #1)
  • Brass & Aluminium – 100% recyclable with high scrap value
  • Wood – biodegradable; can be reused or composted
  • Electronics – WEEE directive; PCB & components need specialist recycling
  • Batteries – must be recycled via dedicated battery collection
  • Overall – ~75% of materials can be recycled or composted
Recycling potential: high for metals & plastics

♻️ End-of-Life Strategy

  • Disassembly – Designed with screws and snap-fits to separate materials easily.
  • Plastics – PLA parts can be industrially composted; PETG sorted and recycled.
  • Metals – Brass and aluminium sent to scrap metal recycling (high recovery rate).
  • Wood – Ash and mahogany can be repurposed or composted if untreated.
  • PCBs & electronics – Collected via WEEE scheme; precious metals recovered.
  • Batteries – Return to retailer or battery recycling point.

✅ Circular potential: Over 70% of the product by mass can be recycled or composted with proper sorting.

🌱 Design Improvements

  • Material substitution – Replace PETG with recycled PLA or bio-based PET.
  • Brass alternative – Use stainless steel or aluminium alloy with lower impact.
  • Wood sourcing – Specify FSC-certified Ash & Mahogany.
  • Modular electronics – Use socketed components for easier repair & upgrade.
  • Battery – Switch to rechargeable Li-ion pack (reduces waste over time).
  • 3D printing – Optimise infill & use 100% recycled filament.
  • Packaging – 100% recycled cardboard with no plastic inserts.

💡 Net impact reduction: Estimated 25–35% lower carbon footprint with these changes.
