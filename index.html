Life Cycle
Assessment cradle to grave

1. Resource Extraction

  • Aluminium: Bauxite mining (West Indies). Open-air storage. Alkaline dust destroys wildlife.
  • Plastics: Crude oil extraction. Offshore drilling. Seismic waves disturb marine life. Oil spills.
High impact

2. Processing

  • Aluminium: Bayer Process (Bauxite → Alumina). Produces red mud waste (1.2t per 1t Al). Electrolysis (high energy, 59% coal in China).
  • Plastics: Distillation → Naphtha → Monomers (Acrylic acid, Ethylene, ABS).
High impact

3. Manufacturing

  • Aluminium: Casting / extrusion into profiles.
  • ABS: Polymerisation, extruded into filament for 3D printing (FDM).
  • Acrylic: Polymerised into sheets for laser cutting.
  • Polyester: Polymerised, extruded into fibres for textiles.
Medium impact

4. Distribution

  • Shipping: Coastal shipping from China. Lowest CO₂ per kg.
  • Negatives: Marine habitat damage, oil spills, ballast water disruption.
Medium impact

5. Use

  • Aluminium: Plasma cutting – toxic metal fumes, nitrogen oxides (acid rain). Filters needed.
  • ABS: 3D printing – high electricity, but 70% less waste than injection moulding.
  • Acrylic: Laser cutting – high energy, fumes requiring extraction.
Medium impact

6. End of Life

  • Aluminium: Highly recyclable (5% energy, 75% still in use, 90% recycled).
  • ABS: Mechanical (shred/melt) and chemical (depolymerisation) recycling.
  • Acrylic: Fully recyclable but often difficult due to processing.
  • Polyester: Mechanical recycling lowers quality; chemical recycling is expensive.
Good potential

End-of-Life Strategy

  • Aluminium: Highly recyclable. Only 5% of the energy needed for primary production is required. 75% of all aluminium ever produced is still in use today.
  • ABS: Can be mechanically recycled (shredding and remelting) or chemically recycled (depolymerisation).
  • Acrylic: Fully recyclable, but the specific processes it has undergone can make it difficult. However, my usage will not hinder its recyclability.
  • Polyester: Much harder to recycle. Mechanical recycling makes it useless as a textile. Chemical recycling is available but expensive and inefficient.

Circular potential: Over 70% of the product by mass can be recycled or composted with proper sorting.

Design Improvements

  • Material substitution: Replace virgin plastics with recycled or bio-based alternatives where possible.
  • Metal sourcing: Increase the use of recycled aluminium to reduce reliance on bauxite mining.
  • Modular design: Design for easy disassembly to separate metals, plastics, and electronics for recycling.
  • Reduce energy use: Optimise 3D printing and laser cutting parameters to minimise electricity consumption.
  • Packaging: Use 100% recycled cardboard with no plastic inserts to reduce waste.

Net impact reduction: Estimated 25-35% lower carbon footprint with these changes.
